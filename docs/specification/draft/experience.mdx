---
title: Required experience
---

Each connection states its experience. The author specifies useful output, latency
target, accepted age, correctness, latency scope, indicative-result permission,
deferred-result permission, final deadline and allowed miss behavior. Latency scope
is warmHits, trafficPercentile or everyRequest. Cache hits cannot certify every
request because cold/expired states exist. A live requirement cannot be silently
weakened to indicative output.

Optional goal fields are `firstAckMs`, `silenceBudgetMs`, `availabilityTarget`,
`duplicateEffectForbidden`, `lostRequestAllowed`, `orderingRequired` and
`priorities`. An absent field means no requirement was declared; an explicit `null`
or `unknown` means the requirement itself is unknown and stays unknown. Duplicate
effects and lost requests are forbidden unless declared acceptable. An
acknowledgement is not useful output.

Priorities are a distinct lexicographic order over `correctness`, `latency`,
`availability`, `simplicity` and `cost`, defaulting to that order. They break ties
only among non-dominated architectures and never trade away a security obligation.

Traffic success is bounded by usable hits times independently evidenced within-target
hit probability when misses are slow. Summing point estimates or percentiles does
not establish that probability. Missing evidence remains a conditional claim.
Warm-hit latency is never a traffic objective.
